## Mathematical Foundations

The imperative to safeguard sensitive longitudinal records necessitates a robust paradigm for privacy-preserving offline reinforcement learning. Conventional transition-level clipping is fundamentally inadequate for sequential decision-making, as it fails to encapsulate the temporal correlations inherent in trajectory data. To rectify this, we enforce trajectory-level differential privacy, formally defining the sensitivity $\Delta$ as the maximum $L_2$-norm difference in gradients between any two adjacent trajectories, $\tau$ and $\tau'$:

$$
\Delta = \max_{\tau, \tau'} \left\| \nabla \ell(\tau) - \nabla \ell(\tau') \right\|_2
$$

From a statistical perspective, bounding this trajectory-level sensitivity is paramount; it ensures that the influence of any single longitudinal record — such as a patient's complete ICU history or a comprehensive financial trading sequence — remains mathematically bounded, thereby circumventing the escalating privacy leakage associated with cumulative transition-level exposures.

Furthermore, our framework seamlessly integrates machine unlearning capabilities. To facilitate the precise and computationally tractable removal of specific trajectories, we employ the Linear Time Stochastic Second-Order Algorithm (LiSSA) for inverse Hessian approximation, denoted as $H_\theta^{-1}\nabla_\theta \ell(\tau_k)$. This is synergistically combined with Sharded, Isolated, Sliced, and Aggregated (SISA) episode sharding, establishing a rigorous mechanism for certified data deletion without necessitating complete model retraining.

## Core Contribution: Why IQL Over CQL?

The integration of differential privacy noise into offline reinforcement learning exposes a critical vulnerability in prevailing conservative methodologies. Conservative Q-Learning (CQL), whilst highly effective in deterministic settings, is notoriously susceptible when privacy-calibrated noise is injected. Due to its architectural reliance on sampling out-of-distribution (OOD) actions to penalise the value function, the superposition of DP noise exacerbates Q-value estimation errors, invariably culminating in catastrophic value explosion.

Conversely, Implicit Q-Learning (IQL) circumvents this vulnerability by exclusively leveraging in-sample (in-distribution) data for policy evaluation. By formulating the value estimation as an expectile regression problem rather than relying on OOD sampling, IQL demonstrates an extraordinary resilience to profound DP gradient perturbations. This architectural constraint intrinsically bounds the propagation of noise, enabling the algorithm to maintain exceptional value function convergence and optimally preserve policy utility even under stringent privacy constraints.
