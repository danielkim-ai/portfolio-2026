## The Fallacy of Transition-level DP

The imperative to safeguard sensitive longitudinal records necessitates a rigorous paradigm for privacy-preserving offline reinforcement learning. Conventional methodologies overwhelmingly rely on transition-level clipping, a frequentist approach that treats each $(s, a, r, s')$ tuple as an independently and identically distributed (i.i.d.) sample. Statistically, this assumption is profoundly flawed when applied to sequential decision-making. By applying noise and clipping iteratively at the transition level, the algorithm fundamentally destroys the intrinsic causal structure and temporal correlations inherent in time-series data. 

To rectify this theoretical inadequacy, we enforce trajectory-level differential privacy. By evaluating and bounding the sensitivity across the entirety of an episode ($\tau$), this framework natively aligns with the principle of *auditable minimisation*. Rather than fracturing a patient's medical history or a proprietary trading sequence into disjointed, statistically meaningless fragments, trajectory-level adjacency ensures that the mathematical influence of the entire longitudinal record is bounded. Formally, we define the trajectory-level sensitivity $\Delta$ as the maximum $L_2$-norm difference in gradients between any two adjacent trajectories, $\tau$ and $\tau'$:

$$
\Delta = \max_{\tau, \tau'} \left\| \nabla \ell(\tau) - \nabla \ell(\tau') \right\|_2
$$

This circumvents the escalating privacy leakage and catastrophic privacy budget accumulation invariably associated with cumulative transition-level exposures. Furthermore, to facilitate the computationally tractable removal of specific trajectories, we employ the Linear Time Stochastic Second-Order Algorithm (LiSSA) for inverse Hessian approximation, denoted as $H_\theta^{-1}\nabla_\theta \ell(\tau_k)$. This establishes a rigorous mechanism for certified data deletion.

## Core Contribution: IQL vs CQL Deep Dive

The integration of differential privacy noise into offline reinforcement learning exposes a critical architectural vulnerability in prevailing conservative methodologies. Conservative Q-Learning (CQL) is notoriously susceptible to profound instability when Rényi Differential Privacy (RDP) noise is injected into the gradient updates. 

Due to its structural reliance on sampling out-of-distribution (OOD) actions to penalise the value function, CQL suffers from severe *Bellman Error Amplification*. Mathematically, the superposition of DP noise onto the artificially constructed OOD target values aggressively exacerbates Q-value estimation errors during the Bellman backup. The noise variance scales catastrophically with the horizon, invariably culminating in a *catastrophic explosion* of the value function and subsequent policy collapse.

Conversely, Implicit Q-Learning (IQL) stands as the ultimate bastion of numerical stability under extreme privacy perturbations. IQL circumvents the OOD vulnerability by exclusively leveraging in-sample (in-distribution) data for policy evaluation. By formulating the value estimation strictly as an *expectile regression* problem, IQL fundamentally alters the underlying optimisation landscape, rendering it remarkably smooth and rigorously bounded. This architectural constraint intrinsically arrests the propagation of noise variance. Consequently, IQL demonstrates an extraordinary resilience to profound DP gradient perturbations, enabling the algorithm to maintain exceptional convergence properties even under stringent privacy constraints.

## Domain Generalisation: Bridging Healthcare and Finance

The robustness of the proposed framework was empirically validated across two fundamentally disparate operational domains: the MIMIC-III healthcare proxy and the FinRL financial proxy. 

The healthcare domain is notoriously characterised by highly stochastic, sparse rewards, where patient physiological trajectories exhibit profound variance. Conversely, the financial domain operates within a high Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) environment, demanding meticulous exploitation of minute alpha signals. Our trajectory-level DP framework, fortified by the numerical stability of IQL, successfully bridged this generalisation gap. It seamlessly protected highly sensitive ICU biometric indicators and proprietary trading strategies alike, without requiring domain-specific algorithmic recalibration.
