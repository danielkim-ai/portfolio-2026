## 1. The Markov Decision Process as a Stochastic System

A Markov Decision Process (MDP) is canonically defined as a 5-tuple $\mathcal{M} = (\mathcal{S}, \mathcal{A}, \mathcal{P}, \mathcal{R}, \gamma)$. From a rigorous probabilistic perspective, an MDP under a fixed policy $\pi(a \mid s)$ induces a discrete-time **Markov Chain** over the state space $\mathcal{S}$. 

The state transition kernel of this induced Markov chain is obtained by marginalising over the actions:
$$
P^\pi(s' \mid s) = \int_{\mathcal{A}} \pi(a \mid s) \mathcal{P}(s' \mid s, a) \, da
$$

This formulation shifts our perspective: rather than viewing RL purely as sequential reward accumulation, we analyse it as the **control of a stochastic process** to shape its long-term distribution.

## 2. Ergodic Condition and the State Visitation Distribution

In infinite-horizon RL, a critical theoretical construct is the **state visitation distribution** $\rho^\pi(s)$. We define the discounted visitation frequency as:
$$
\rho^\pi(s) = (1-\gamma) \sum_{t=0}^{\infty} \gamma^t P(s_t = s \mid s_0, \pi)
$$
This quantity is fundamental because the RL objective can be perfectly rewritten as an expectation over this stationary-like distribution:
$$
J(\pi) = \frac{1}{1-\gamma} \mathbb{E}_{s \sim \rho^\pi, a \sim \pi}[r(s,a)]
$$

### The Ergodicity Requirement

For the un-discounted infinite-horizon setting ($\gamma \to 1$), $\rho^\pi$ converges to the true **stationary distribution** $d^\pi(s)$ of the Markov Chain. This convergence is guaranteed by the **Ergodic Theorem** if the Markov Chain is:
1. **Irreducible**: Every state is reachable from every other state (no absorbing traps that isolate sub-graphs).
2. **Aperiodic**: The chain does not oscillate deterministically between sets of states.
3. **Positive Recurrent**: The expected return time to any state is finite.

When these ergodic conditions are satisfied, there exists a unique stationary distribution $d^\pi$ satisfying the balance equation:
$$
d^\pi(s') = \int_{\mathcal{S}} P^\pi(s' \mid s) d^\pi(s) \, ds
$$

### Statistical Implications for RL

Viewing RL through the lens of ergodicity reveals deep insights:
- **Exploration vs. Stationarity**: If the environment is non-ergodic (e.g., contains terminal states or unconnected components), the initial state distribution $p(s_0)$ permanently biases learning. Ergodicity guarantees that the long-term behaviour is independent of the starting state.
- **Off-Policy Evaluation Bias**: When we evaluate a target policy $\pi$ using data collected by a behaviour policy $\mu$, we face a mismatch not just in action probabilities, but in the structural stationary distributions: $\rho^\pi \neq \rho^\mu$. Correcting for this marginal density ratio $w(s) = \rho^\pi(s)/\rho^\mu(s)$ is one of the profound challenges in modern statistical RL.
