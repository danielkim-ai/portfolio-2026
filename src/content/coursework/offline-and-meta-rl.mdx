## 1. Offline RL and the Covariate Shift Problem

Offline Reinforcement Learning seeks to derive an optimal policy $\pi$ strictly from a static dataset $\mathcal{D}$ generated by an unknown behaviour policy $\mu$. In classical machine learning, assuming independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) data is standard. However, Offline RL fundamentally breaks this assumption, inducing a phenomenon known mathematically as **Covariate Shift**.

In supervised learning, Covariate Shift occurs when the training distribution of inputs $p_\text{train}(x)$ differs from the test distribution $p_\text{test}(x)$, while the conditional expectation $p(y \mid x)$ remains constant. In Offline RL, the covariates are the state-action pairs. The dataset is drawn from the marginal distribution of the behaviour policy $d^\mu(s,a)$, but the objective is to evaluate the Q-function under the distribution induced by the learned policy $d^\pi(s,a)$.

Because deep neural networks aggressively extrapolate outside their training support, querying the Q-function at states outside $d^\mu(s,a)$ produces unboundedly erroneous predictions.

---

## 2. Importance Sampling and Asymptotic Variance

To mathematically correct for Covariate Shift without environment interaction, one might apply **Importance Sampling (IS)** to re-weight the dataset trajectories:
$$
J(\pi) = \mathbb{E}_{\tau \sim \mu} \left[ \left( \prod_{t=0}^T \frac{\pi(a_t \mid s_t)}{\mu(a_t \mid s_t)} \right) R(\tau) \right]
$$

While this estimator is statistically unbiased (i.e., its expected value is exactly the true performance of $\pi$), its **variance** is catastrophic. The variance of the IS estimator scales exponentially with the horizon length $T$:
$$
\text{Var}(\hat{J}) \propto \mathbb{E}_{\tau \sim \mu} \left[ \left( \prod_{t=0}^T \frac{\pi(a_t \mid s_t)}{\mu(a_t \mid s_t)} \right)^2 \right] - J(\pi)^2
$$

When $\pi$ deviates significantly from $\mu$, the product of density ratios frequently approaches zero, but occasionally spikes to astronomical values, leading to an infinite variance estimator in practice. This fundamental statistical limit proves that pure off-policy evaluation via IS is inherently intractable for long-horizon Offline RL.

---

## 3. Conservative Value Regularisation as a Statistical Prior

Because unbiased IS estimation is bounded by variance, modern Offline RL abandons unbiasedness in favour of controlled statistical bias. **Conservative Q-Learning (CQL)** injects a strong structural prior into the learning process.

By augmenting the Bellman objective with a conservatism penalty:
$$
J_\text{CQL}(Q) = \alpha_\text{CQL} \left( \mathbb{E}_{s \sim \mathcal{D}, a \sim \pi}[Q(s,a)] - \mathbb{E}_{(s,a) \sim \mathcal{D}}[Q(s,a)] \right) + \mathcal{L}_\text{Bellman}(Q)
$$
CQL forces the neural network to systematically under-estimate the Q-values of Out-of-Distribution (OOD) actions.

Statistically, this can be interpreted as imposing an **asymmetric Gaussian Prior** over the neural network's predictions. Instead of allowing the variance of OOD predictions to explode symmetrically, the regularisation truncates the upper tail of the prediction distribution, ensuring that the optimistic extrapolation error inherent in Covariate Shift is strictly bounded from above. This converts an intractable variance problem into a tractable bias problem, enabling safe offline policy extraction.
