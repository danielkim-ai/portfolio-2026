# STA3123: SAMPLING THEORY IN DATA SCIENCE

## "Bridging Classical Survey Methods and Privacy-Preserving Reinforcement Learning"

This document fuses the core mathematical foundations of classical Sampling Theory with the modern statistical challenges of Offline Reinforcement Learning (RL) and Differential Privacy (DP). We interpret deeply how finite population methodologies directly underpin advanced machine unlearning and privacy amplification dynamics.

---

## 1. Foundations of Finite Population Inference

### Classical Theory: Finite Population and Sampling Design
In classical sampling theory, we aim to estimate population parameters (e.g., the population total $t_y = \sum_{i=1}^N y_i$) of a strictly **Finite Population** $U = \{1, 2, \dots, N\}$ using a smaller sample $S$ of size $n$. The defining characteristic of finite population inference is that the values $y_i$ are treated as fixed, deterministic constants; the only source of randomness arises from the **Sampling Design** $p(S)$, which assigns a probability to every possible subset $S \subset U$. 

### Research Connection: RL Datasets and DP Adjacency
In **Offline RL**, the pre-collected logged dataset $\mathcal{D}$ is fundamentally a finite population of trajectories. Rather than treating trajectories as independent samples from an infinite generating distribution, we lock the empirical dataset as our finite population $U$. 

This perspective is crucial for **Differential Privacy (DP)**. When we define dataset adjacency in DP, we typically assume the addition or removal of a single user's record. If our sampling design uses **Sampling Without Replacement (SWOR)** to form mini-batches, the covariance structure between sampled trajectories changes drastically compared to sampling with replacement (SWR). SWOR structurally constraints the sample size to exactly $n$, tightening the variance. In terms of DP, sampling without replacement alters the theoretical bound of the $L_2$-sensitivity ($\Delta f$), directly governing how much Laplacian or Gaussian noise is required to satisfy $\epsilon$-DP constraints.

## 2. Estimation Theory & Horvitz-Thompson

### Classical Theory: HT Estimator, Unbiasedness, and Variance
The **Horvitz-Thompson (HT) Estimator** allows for unbiased estimation under any arbitrary unequal probability sampling design. Let $\pi_i = P(i \in S)$ be the first-order inclusion probability. The HT estimator for the population total is:
$$
\hat{t}_{\text{HT}} = \sum_{i \in S} \frac{y_i}{\pi_i}
$$
The estimator's unbiasedness is proven by taking the expectation over the sampling design indicator $I_i$ (where $I_i = 1$ if $i \in S$, and $0$ otherwise, with $\mathbb{E}_p[I_i] = \pi_i$):
$$
\mathbb{E}_p[\hat{t}_{\text{HT}}] = \mathbb{E}_p \left[ \sum_{i \in U} I_i \frac{y_i}{\pi_i} \right] = \sum_{i \in U} \frac{y_i}{\pi_i} \mathbb{E}_p[I_i] = \sum_{i=1}^N y_i = t_y
$$
The exact design variance incorporates the second-order inclusion probabilities $\pi_{ij} = P(i, j \in S)$:
$$
V(\hat{t}_{\text{HT}}) = \sum_{i=1}^N \sum_{j=1}^N (\pi_{ij} - \pi_i \pi_j) \frac{y_i}{\pi_i} \frac{y_j}{\pi_j}
$$

### Research Connection: Privacy Amplification via Subsampling
In our privacy-preserving DP-SGD pipelines, the HT weighting mechanism $1/\pi_i$ directly corresponds to the **Subsampling Ratio ($q$)**. 

When a mini-batch is drawn with an inclusion probability $\pi_i = q$, the privacy cost is not computed on the full $\epsilon$ of the update, but rather on an amplified budget. By the **Privacy Amplification via Subsampling** theorem, an algorithm that is $\epsilon$-DP on the full dataset becomes exactly $\ln(1 + q(e^\epsilon - 1))$-DP when evaluated on a subsample. Thus, $\pi_i$ dictates not only the variance of our RL objective estimators (like Importance Sampling in OPE), but rigorously sets the mathematical boundary for our privacy leakage profile over recursive Bellman backups.

## 3. Cluster Sampling & SISA (Sharding)

### Classical Theory: Intra-cluster Correlation and Design Effect
In **Cluster Sampling**, the population is partitioned into clusters (Primary Sampling Units). If clusters are internally homogeneous but heterogeneous from one another, the **Intra-cluster Correlation Coefficient (ICC)**, denoted as $\rho$, becomes highly positive. 
This spatial similarity destroys the statistical efficiency of the sample. The variance inflates according to the **Design Effect (Deff)**:
$$
\text{Deff} = 1 + (m - 1)\rho
$$
where $m$ is the cluster size. A large $\rho$ heavily degrades estimation performance compared to Simple Random Sampling (SRS).

### Research Connection: Sharding and Temporal Coherence Loss
This statistical penalty directly translates to the architectural vulnerabilities of **SISA (Sharded, Isolated, Sliced, and Aggregated)** in Machine Unlearning. 

To unlearn a user's trajectory without full retraining, SISA partitions the Offline RL dataset into isolated shards (clusters). However, RL trajectories possess strict **Temporal Coherence** (Markovian transitions $s_t \to s_{t+1}$). If an episode is naively fragmented and scattered across different shards, the intra-cluster correlation is obliterated, destroying the temporal structure essential for the Bellman operator. From a sampling theory perspective, this sharding induces a catastrophic **Design Effect loss**, structurally bounding how well the aggregated ensemble models can approximate the optimal Q-function.

## 4. Non-sampling Errors & Machine Unlearning

### Classical Theory: Non-response and Weighting Adjustment
**Non-sampling errors** arise not from the random selection process, but from systematic failures in data collection. The most prominent is **Non-response**, where selected units do not provide data. If the missingness mechanism is non-ignorable, the sample mean becomes a biased estimator of the population mean.
Survey methodologists correct this using **Weighting Adjustments** (e.g., Propensity Score Weighting) or **Imputation**, recalculating weights of respondent units to compensate for the missing strata and statistically restoring the population's true distribution.

### Research Connection: Unlearning via Influence Functions
In Privacy and Compliance (e.g., GDPR), a user invoking the **Right to be Forgotten** equates mathematically to a post-hoc induced **Survey Non-response**. The trajectory is permanently erased from the dataset $\mathcal{D}$.

Rather than paying the computationally intractable cost of retraining the RL model from scratch, we frame this as a statistical weighting adjustment problem. Using the **Influence Function**, we approximate how the optimal model parameters $\theta^*$ would shift if the deleted trajectory were heavily down-weighted (or zeroed out). By applying an inverse-Hessian vector product, we can execute a gradient-based update that statistically neutralises the unlearned data's influence, efficiently restoring the global policy's integrity just as survey statisticians impute non-response errors.
