## 1. Surrogate Objective as Importance Sampling

In standard policy gradients, estimating the performance of a new policy $\pi_\theta$ directly from trajectories collected under an old policy $\pi_{\theta_\text{old}}$ is a classic **off-policy evaluation** problem. Statistically, this is resolved using **Importance Sampling (IS)**.

The expected return under the new policy is:
$$
J(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{\tau \sim \pi_\theta}[R(\tau)] = \int p_\theta(\tau) R(\tau) \, d\tau
$$

By multiplying and dividing by the trajectory distribution of the old policy $p_{\theta_\text{old}}(\tau)$, we obtain the IS estimator:
$$
J(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{\tau \sim \pi_{\theta_\text{old}}} \left[ \frac{p_\theta(\tau)}{p_{\theta_\text{old}}(\tau)} R(\tau) \right]
$$

Since the transition dynamics $\mathcal{P}$ cancel out in the probability ratio, the ratio isolates the policy parameters:
$$
\frac{p_\theta(\tau)}{p_{\theta_\text{old}}(\tau)} = \prod_{t=0}^T \frac{\pi_\theta(a_t \mid s_t)}{\pi_{\theta_\text{old}}(a_t \mid s_t)}
$$

However, a product of IS weights over time suffers from **exponential variance growth**. TRPO mitigates this by applying IS marginally at the state-action level, substituting the full return with the Advantage function $A^{\pi_{\theta_\text{old}}}$, yielding the celebrated **Surrogate Objective**:
$$
\mathcal{L}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{s \sim \rho^{\pi_{\theta_\text{old}}}, a \sim \pi_{\theta_\text{old}}} \left[ \frac{\pi_\theta(a \mid s)}{\pi_{\theta_\text{old}}(a \mid s)} A^{\pi_{\theta_\text{old}}}(s,a) \right]
$$

This objective is valid statistically only in a small local neighbourhood where the importance weights are well-behaved.

## 2. Information Geometry and the Natural Gradient

To enforce this local neighbourhood, TRPO restricts the update by bounding the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence. Why KL divergence instead of Euclidean distance $\|\theta - \theta_\text{old}\|_2^2$? This is a profound question answered by **Information Geometry**.

Parameter space (Euclidean) is not isomorphic to probability distribution space. A small step $\Delta\theta$ in Euclidean space can cause a drastically large shift in the actual probability distribution $\pi_\theta$, leading to catastrophic IS variance. 

### The Fisher Information Metric

Information Geometry maps probability distributions as manifolds. The local curvature of this manifold is defined by the **Fisher Information Matrix (FIM)** $F_\theta$, which acts as the Riemannian metric tensor:
$$
D_{\text{KL}}(\pi_\theta \,\|\, \pi_{\theta + \Delta\theta}) \approx \frac{1}{2} \Delta\theta^\top F_\theta \Delta\theta
$$
where $F_\theta = \mathbb{E}_{\pi_\theta} [\nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta^\top]$.

### The Natural Gradient Update

By performing constrained optimisation in this Riemannian manifold, we find the steepest ascent direction that maximally improves the surrogate objective whilst ensuring a bounded change in the probability distribution. This yields the **Natural Gradient**:
$$
\Delta\theta \propto F_\theta^{-1} \nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}(\theta)
$$

The Natural Gradient fundamentally uncouples the learning dynamics from the arbitrary parameterisation of the neural network, ensuring that the optimization takes invariant, statistically consistent steps across the manifold of policy distributions.
