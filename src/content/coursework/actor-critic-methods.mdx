## 1. Reinforcement Learning as Probabilistic Inference

Standard Reinforcement Learning focuses on the deterministic maximisation of cumulative rewards. However, we can construct an equivalent probabilistic graphical model by introducing an auxiliary optimality variable $\mathcal{O}_t$. We define the probability of a transition being optimal as exponential in the reward:
$$
p(\mathcal{O}_t = 1 \mid s_t, a_t) = \exp(r(s_t, a_t))
$$

The goal of the agent shifts from pure maximisation to **posterior inference**: computing the optimal trajectory distribution $p(\tau \mid \mathcal{O}_{1:T} = 1)$. Because exactly computing this posterior is intractable in complex MDPs, we must resort to **Variational Inference (VI)**.

---

## 2. Entropy Regularisation as Variational Inference

In VI, we approximate the true intractable posterior $p(\tau \mid \mathcal{O})$ with a tractable variational distribution, which in RL corresponds to our parameterised policy $q_\theta(\tau)$. The inference problem reduces to minimising the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between $q_\theta$ and $p$:

$$
D_{\text{KL}}(q_\theta(\tau) \,\|\, p(\tau \mid \mathcal{O})) = \mathbb{E}_{q_\theta} [\log q_\theta(\tau) - \log p(\tau \mid \mathcal{O})]
$$

By algebraically expanding this KL divergence, we recover the **Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO)**. Maximising the ELBO yields precisely the **Maximum Entropy RL** objective used in Soft Actor-Critic (SAC):

$$
J(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{q_\theta} \left[ \sum_t \gamma^t \left( r(s_t, a_t) + \alpha \mathcal{H}(q_\theta(a_t \mid s_t)) \right) \right]
$$

Here, $\mathcal{H}(q_\theta) = -\log q_\theta$ is the Shannon entropy. The entropy bonus is not merely an ad-hoc heuristic for exploration; it is the fundamental mathematical consequence of applying Variational Inference to the RL graphical model.

---

## 3. Probability Density Spreading and the Temperature $\alpha$

The **temperature parameter $\alpha$** controls the statistical dispersion (spreading) of the policy's probability density function over the action space. 

### Topological Spreading and Mode Collapse

Consider an environment with multiple equivalent optimal actions (a multi-modal reward landscape). A purely deterministic policy (where $\alpha \to 0$) mathematically behaves as a Dirac delta function $\delta(a - a^*)$, collapsing onto a single mode and discarding all alternative probability mass. This phenomenon is analogous to **Mode Collapse** in Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs).

Conversely, under strict entropy regularisation ($\alpha > 0$):
- **Density Spreading**: The policy is forced to allocate probability mass proportional to $\exp(\frac{1}{\alpha} Q(s,a))$. 
- **Robustness (Epistemic Uncertainty)**: By maintaining a broad probability density, the agent intrinsically models epistemic uncertainty. When deployed, if the primary optimum becomes perturbed by environmental noise, the widespread density ensures that alternative optimal pathways remain inherently active and immediately traversable.

### Automatic Temperature Tuning as Dual Optimisation

SAC dynamically adjusts $\alpha$ via dual gradient descent to maintain a specific target entropy $\bar{\mathcal{H}}$:
$$
J(\alpha) = \mathbb{E}_{a \sim \pi} [-\alpha \log \pi(a \mid s) - \alpha \bar{\mathcal{H}}]
$$
Statistically, this corresponds to constraining the marginal entropy of the variational distribution, ensuring that the continuous density function never collapses prematurely during the inference process.
